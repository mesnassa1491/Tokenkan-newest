1. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

1.1 Overview of the Issuer

MediQ Peranti Bhd manufactures single-use medical consumables and diagnostic devices at a purpose-built cleanroom facility it owns at
Kulim, Kedah. It manufactures under an international brand's label as an original equipment manufacturer, sells its own diagnostic range
to Malaysian hospital groups and clinics, and provides contract manufacturing services.

The Issuer has been listed on the ACE Market of Bursa Malaysia since February 2024 and had a market capitalisation of RM52.6 million as
at 30 June 2026. It employed 212 persons as at 31 December 2025.

For the financial year ended 31 December 2025 the Issuer recorded revenue of RM35.4 million, EBITDA of RM7.36 million and profit for
the financial year of RM2.04 million. Total assets stood at RM54.5 million and shareholders' funds at RM19.3 million.

1.2 Purpose of the offering

The Issuer is offering RM3,500,000 in nominal value of Secured Sukuk Murabahah, the inaugural issue under its RM15.0 million Sukuk
Programme. Of that, RM2,300,000 is allocated to the Anchor Investor and RM1,200,000 to retail investors through the Tokenkan platform
in fractional denominations from RM100, subject to a maximum holding of RM8,000 per investor.

Proceeds will fund cleanroom expansion and injection moulding equipment, a regulatory approval and certification programme, refinancing
and working capital. Section 5 has the detail.

1.3 The smallest issue on the Platform, and what follows from that

At RM3.5 million this is the smallest offering on the Platform, and the profit rate of 7.50 per cent. is the highest. Both facts follow from the
same cause.

A conventional RM3.5 million bond issuance is impossible in Malaysia: fixed costs of RM1.4 million to RM1.9 million would consume 40 to
54 per cent. of the proceeds. Even a credit rating, at RM150,000 to RM200,000, would take four to six per cent. The total cost of this
issuance is RM155,000, being 4.43 per cent. of nominal value, achieved by removing the underwriter, declining a rating, and standardising
the documentation.

Investors should understand what they accept in exchange: no rating, no underwriter with capital at risk, a company with a two-year public
record, and a business concentrated on one large customer. The compensation is 7.50 per cent. and the best security on the Platform.

1.4 What protects investors here

The security is the substantive answer, and it is stronger than on most small issues:

    A freehold factory, professionally valued. The Kulim premises are valued at RM26.4 million and carry RM9.6 million of prior-ranking
    financing, leaving RM16.8 million of equity against RM3.5 million of Sukuk. That is 4.80 times cover, against a covenanted minimum of
    1.30 times.
    A debenture over plant, equipment, inventories and receivables.
    An assignment of RM18.4 million of annual receivables from the principal OEM customer and the three largest hospital distributors,
    payable into a Trustee-controlled account on default.
    A corporate guarantee from the controlling shareholder.
    Two mandatory redemption triggers tied to the events most likely to end the business: loss of regulatory approvals for more than
    ninety days, and loss of the principal OEM customer without substantial replacement.

Investors should discount the valuation for the specialised nature of a cleanroom facility and for the fact that a valuation assumes the plant
remains regulator-certified. Section 4.6 sets out both discounts candidly.

2. ISSUER BACKGROUND

2.1 History and development

The Issuer was incorporated in September 2018 by Dr. Tan Chee Wei, a biomedical engineer who had spent eleven years with a
multinational medical device manufacturer, to produce single-use consumables in Malaysia for regional and export markets.

It acquired the Kulim site in 2019 and commissioned its first cleanroom in 2020. The OEM supply agreement with its principal customer
was signed in 2021 and has been renewed once, currently running to December 2028; that agreement provided the volume base on which
the Issuer built its capacity. The own-brand diagnostic range was launched in 2022 and ISO 13485 certification obtained the same year.

The Issuer listed on the ACE Market in February 2024, raising RM11.8 million, of which RM8.4 million funded the second cleanroom and
moulding capacity. This is its inaugural debt issuance.

Investors should note that the operating history of the business is seven years and the public reporting history two.

2.2 Business model

The Issuer earns revenue in three ways, with materially different characteristics.

OEM manufacture, at RM21.4 million in FY2025, involves producing consumables to a customer's specification under that customer's label.
Volumes are large and predictable while the agreement runs, margins are thinner than on own-brand product, and the relationship is
concentrated in one customer.

Own-brand diagnostic devices, at RM9.8 million, are sold to Malaysian hospital groups and clinics through the distribution subsidiary.
Margins are higher, the customer base is more diversified across thirteen hospital groups, but the sales cycle is longer and each product
requires its own registration.

Contract manufacturing, at RM3.6 million, uses spare cleanroom capacity for smaller customers and is the most variable of the three.

The strategic direction is toward own-brand product, because it diversifies the customer base and improves margin. That shift requires
regulatory approvals, which is what RM620,000 of this issue funds, and it is slow: own-brand share of revenue has moved only from 27.6
per cent. to 27.7 per cent. over three years, because OEM volumes grew in parallel.

The Sukuk are structured as a single bullet maturity. The full nominal value of RM3,500,000 falls due on 18 November 2029 together with

What the security is realistically worth
The factory charge is the substantive protection and the arithmetic is more favourable than investors might expect on an issue of this size.
The property is valued at RM26.4 million and carries existing Islamic term financing of RM9.6 million ranking ahead, leaving RM16.8
million of equity against RM3.5 million of Sukuk, a cover of 4.8 times.

Investors should nonetheless apply two discounts to that figure. First, a purpose-built cleanroom facility is a specialised asset with a limited
pool of buyers, so a forced sale would realise materially below an existing use valuation. Second, the valuation assumes the facility remains
certified and operable; a facility that has lost its regulatory approvals is worth considerably less, and the circumstances producing a default
would often be the same circumstances that cost the Issuer its certification.

Even after both discounts, cover of 4.8 times before them gives more room than most small issues provide, and the security covenant in
Section 4.8 requires it to remain above 1.30 times.

4.7 Ranking of investors

The Sukuk constitute direct, unconditional and unsubordinated obligations of the Issuer, secured as described in Section 4.6 and ranking
pari passu among themselves.

On the factory premises, the Sukuk rank behind existing Islamic term financing of RM9.6 million and ahead of all unsecured creditors.
Under the debenture, the Sukuk rank ahead of unsecured creditors in respect of the plant, equipment, inventories and receivables
charged, subject to the prior claims of the equipment financiers described in Section 6.6 over the specific equipment they financed.

In respect of any shortfall not recovered from the security, Sukukholders would rank as unsecured creditors, behind winding-up costs and
preferential claims including employee entitlements. The Issuer employed 212 persons as at 31 December 2025.

Fractional interests rank equally with whole units and with the Anchor Investor allocation.

7.1 Risks relating to the Issuer and the Sukuk

Credit risk

The Sukuk represent unsecured or, where applicable, secured obligations of the Issuer alone. Payment of profit and the repayment of the
nominal value depend entirely upon the ability of the Issuer to generate sufficient cash flow from its operations, or to obtain sufficient
funds from other sources, at the relevant times. Neither the Trustee, the Platform through which this offering was originated and
distributed, nor any other party has guaranteed or assumed any obligation in respect of the payment obligations of the Issuer. In
particular, investors should note that this offering was not underwritten and that no arranger stands behind it.

A deterioration in the operating performance of the Issuer, an adverse change in its capital structure, the loss of one or more significant
customers or contracts, or an unexpected increase in its cost base could impair its ability to service the Sukuk. As at 31 December 2025,
the Group had total borrowings of RM19.8 million against total equity of RM19.3 million, representing a gross gearing ratio of 1.03 times
and total borrowings to EBITDA of 2.7 times.

Refinancing risk

The Sukuk are repayable in a single bullet payment at maturity, and the Issuer does not expect to accumulate a sinking fund sufficient to
retire the full nominal amount from operating cash flow alone. The Issuer therefore expects to refinance all or a substantial part of the
Sukuk at or before maturity. The ability of the Issuer to refinance will depend on conditions in the Malaysian debt capital and bank loan
markets at the relevant time, on its credit standing and rating at that time, and on investor appetite for the sector in which it operates.
There is no assurance that refinancing will be available on acceptable terms, or at all.

Profit rate risk

The Sukuk carry a fixed profit rate. The market value of fixed-rate debt securities generally moves inversely with prevailing market yields:
if benchmark yields, including the yields on Malaysian Government Securities and Government Investment Issues, rise after the issue date,
the market value of the Sukuk is likely to fall. Investors who sell the Sukuk before maturity may therefore realise less than the nominal
value, and in adverse conditions materially less. Investors who hold the Sukuk to maturity are not exposed to this price risk, but bear the
opportunity cost of being locked into a fixed profit rate for the tenure of the instrument.

The Issuer is separately exposed to profit rate risk on its floating-rate bank borrowings, which are typically priced against the relevant cost
of funds of the lending institutions. An increase in the Overnight Policy Rate set by the central bank would increase the finance costs of the
Group and reduce the headroom available under its profit cover covenant.

Liquidity risk of the Sukuk

The Sukuk will not be listed or quoted on any stock exchange, and the Platform does not operate an order book. Operating a venue that
matches buyers and sellers of capital market products would require registration as a Recognized Market Operator, which the Platform has
not yet obtained. Exit before maturity is therefore available only through the standing bid maintained by the Anchor Investor at the
published formula price described in Section 9.5. That bid is a contractual undertaking of a single counterparty, not a market. It is capped
at RM60,000 of nominal value in aggregate in any calendar month, may be suspended in the circumstances set out in Section 9.5, and
terminates if the Anchor Investor ceases to hold at least half of its original allocation. Peer-to-peer transfer between investors is intended
to follow in phase two once Recognized Market Operator registration is obtained, but no date is committed and investors should not
subscribe in reliance on it. An investor who may need the money before 18 November 2029 should treat this offering as unsuitable.

Liquidity and working capital risk of the Issuer

The Issuer requires liquidity to fund its operations, service its debt and meet its capital commitments. As at 31 December 2025, the Group
held cash and bank balances of RM4.6 million and recorded a current ratio of 1.26 times. An unexpected lengthening of the collection
cycle, the withdrawal of uncommitted banking lines, or a requirement to post additional performance security could strain the liquidity
position of the Group.

Rating risk

No rating agency has been appointed and these Sukuk are unrated. Investors therefore have no independent professional opinion on the
creditworthiness of the Issuer, no continuous surveillance of that opinion, and no downgrade signal to warn them of deterioration. On a
rated issue, a rating agency reviews the credit at least annually and will place it on watch or downgrade it if conditions worsen, and that
signal is public. Here there is no such mechanism. Investors depend instead on the security described in Section 4.6, on the semi-annual
covenant compliance certificates delivered to the Trustee under Section 4.8, on the quarterly reporting the Issuer has undertaken to
publish through the Platform, and on the continuing disclosure obligations applicable to it as an ACE Market listed company. Those are
materially less than a rating provides, and investors should weigh the fact that the Issuer has been subject to those listing obligations only
since February 2024.

7.2 Risks relating to the industry and operations of the Issuer

Single OEM customer concentration
The principal OEM customer accounted for RM14.8 million, or 41.8 per cent., of FY2025 revenue. The Issuer manufactures single-use
consumables to that customer's specification and under its label, and the current supply agreement runs to December 2028, eleven months
before these Sukuk mature.

Renewal is at the discretion of the customer and depends on price, quality performance and the customer's own sourcing strategy,
including any decision to consolidate manufacturing or shift it to a lower-cost jurisdiction. Loss of the agreement would remove more than
two fifths of revenue and would leave the Issuer with cleanroom capacity built for that volume.

Investors have the mandatory redemption mechanism in Section 4.5 and the receivables assignment in Section 4.6, but neither prevents
the loss, and a redemption obligation is only as good as the ability of the Issuer to pay it in that scenario.

Regulatory approval as a precondition of trading
Unlike most manufacturers, the Issuer cannot sell anything without regulatory approval. It requires a Medical Device Authority
establishment licence, product registration for each device, and ISO 13485 certification to supply its OEM customer and to export.

The consequence is that a quality system failure is not merely a cost but a stoppage. A suspension of certification halts supply to the OEM
customer immediately and would likely end that relationship regardless of whether the suspension was later lifted.

Product liability and recall
A defect in a single-use medical consumable can cause patient harm, and the Issuer bears liability for the products it manufactures even
where they carry another party's label, subject to the terms of its supply agreements.

A recall would involve the cost of retrieval and replacement, potential claims, regulatory investigation, and damage to the OEM
relationship. Insurance cover of RM20.0 million per occurrence exceeds the size of this issue but is not unlimited and would not protect the
commercial relationship.

New product and export approval risk
RM620,000 of the proceeds funds a regulatory programme including United States Food and Drug Administration 510(k) clearance for a
diagnostic device. Such processes typically take twelve to twenty-four months, may require additional testing, and can fail.

The commercial case for the cleanroom expansion assumes those approvals are obtained. If they are not, the Issuer will have added
capacity without the market access to fill it, and the expenditure would produce no return.

Raw material price and supply volatility

The Issuer converts medical-grade polymers, principally polypropylene and polycarbonate, into finished devices. Those inputs are
petrochemical derivatives whose prices move with oil and with global capacity, and medical-grade specification limits the pool of qualified
suppliers.

Supply agreements with the OEM customer contain limited pass-through of input cost increases and are repriced annually rather than
continuously, so a mid-year input cost rise is absorbed by the Issuer. Inventories of RM9.2 million as at 31 December 2025 also expose the
Issuer to price declines.

Competition from lower-cost regional manufacturers

Single-use consumable manufacture is contestable, and the Issuer competes against manufacturers in China, Vietnam and India with lower
labour and overhead costs. Its defence is proximity to customers, ISO certification, and a quality record, none of which is permanent.
