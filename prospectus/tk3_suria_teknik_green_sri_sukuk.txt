1. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

1.1 Overview of the Issuer

Suria Teknik Bhd designs and installs solar photovoltaic systems for commercial and industrial customers in Malaysia, and owns and
operates a portfolio of 46 rooftop installations totalling 28 MWac from which it sells energy under corporate power purchase agreements.
The Issuer has been listed on the ACE Market of Bursa Malaysia since March 2023 and had a market capitalisation of RM94.8 million as at
30 June 2026. It employed 168 persons as at 31 December 2025.
For the financial year ended 31 December 2025 the Issuer recorded revenue of RM94.2 million, EBITDA of RM15.38 million and profit for
the financial year of RM6.20 million. Total assets stood at RM109.1 million and shareholders' funds at RM36.8 million.

1.2 Purpose of the offering

The Issuer is offering RM8,000,000 in nominal value of Green SRI Sukuk Murabahah, the inaugural issue under its RM40.0 million Green
Sukuk Programme. Of that, RM5,200,000 is allocated to the Anchor Investor and RM2,800,000 to retail investors through the Tokenkan
platform in fractional denominations from RM100, subject to a maximum holding of RM15,000 per investor.
Proceeds will fund the construction of a 12 MWac owned rooftop portfolio, working capital for the engineering order book, refinancing and
general working capital. Section 5 has the detail.

1.3 What this Issuer actually is

The name and the green label both suggest an energy company. Two thirds of the revenue says otherwise, and investors should be clear
about the distinction before subscribing.
In FY2025 the Issuer earned RM62.8 million, being 66.7 per cent. of revenue, from installing solar systems for third parties as an
engineering contractor. It earned RM24.6 million, being 26.1 per cent., from selling energy generated by systems it owns.
Those businesses carry opposite risks. Contracting is cash-generative and low-capital but lives on an order book that must be continually
replenished; the order book stood at RM48.4 million at year end, roughly nine months of contracting revenue. Owned energy assets are
capital-hungry but produce contracted revenue for eight to eighteen years.
This issue funds a deliberate shift toward the second: RM4.6 million builds a 12 MWac owned portfolio. Investors are financing that
transition and bearing the contracting risk while it takes place. The portfolio will not be fully commissioned before 2029; profit payments
begin in February 2027.

1.4 Why this issuance exists

A conventional RM8.0 million bond issuance is not economic. Fixed costs of RM1.4 million to RM1.9 million, largely irrespective of size,
would consume 18 to 24 per cent. of the proceeds. The total cost of this issuance is RM240,000, being 3.0 per cent. of nominal value.

1.5 Key investment highlights

Strong revenue growth from a real market
Revenue grew 26.0 per cent. and 19.8 per cent. over the last two years on genuine commercial and industrial demand for rooftop solar,
driven by corporate emissions commitments and electricity tariff levels.

Margin improving as owned assets grow
The EBITDA margin rose from 12.9 to 16.3 per cent. as higher-margin owned energy sales displaced contracting in the mix.

Contracted energy revenue of long duration
The existing 28 MWac portfolio sells under agreements of eight to eighteen years remaining, across 38 separate offtakers, limiting the
effect of any single loss.

Moderate leverage with covenant headroom
Gross gearing of 1.21 times against a 1.75 times covenant, and financing to EBITDA of 2.9 times against 4.50 times.

Green framework with independent assessment
Application of proceeds is assessed against the SRI framework of the Issuer by an independent green assessor, with annual reporting
through the Platform.

2. ISSUER BACKGROUND

2.1 History and development

The Issuer was incorporated in October 2017 by Lau Wei Sheng, who had spent the preceding decade in electrical contracting and
renewable project development, to install rooftop solar for commercial and industrial customers as Malaysian net energy metering
programmes opened.

The Issuer grew initially as a pure contractor. It began retaining ownership of installations in 2021, funding them with project financing
and selling the energy to the building occupier under a corporate power purchase agreement, an arrangement that suits customers
unwilling to fund a solar system themselves. It listed on the ACE Market in March 2023, raising RM18.6 million, of which RM12.4 million
funded owned installations.

The owned portfolio has grown from 16 MWac at the end of FY2023 to 28 MWac at the end of FY2025. This is the inaugural debt issuance
of the Issuer.

2.2 Business model

The Issuer operates two businesses that share an engineering capability.

In the first, it designs, procures and installs a solar system for a customer who pays for it, earning a contracting margin on delivery. This
requires little capital, generates cash on completion, and depends entirely on winning the next contract.

In the second, it builds a system at its own cost on a customer's roof and sells the energy produced to that customer under an agreement of
typically fifteen to twenty years at a tariff below the prevailing grid rate. This requires substantial capital, produces contracted revenue for
many years, and is funded with project financing secured on the installation.

The strategic logic of the second model is that it converts a one-off contracting margin into a long-dated income stream, which is what a
credit investor values. The strategic difficulty is that it consumes capital the Issuer does not generate internally, which is the purpose of
this issue.

2.5 Management and employees

The Issuer employed 168 persons as at 31 December 2025, of whom 96 were installation and site personnel, 28 in engineering and design,
22 in operation and maintenance, and 22 in corporate functions. Installation labour is supplemented by subcontractors during peak
periods.

2.6 Strategy

The strategy is to shift the revenue mix toward owned energy assets while maintaining the contracting business that generates the
customer relationships those assets depend on. Management targets owned energy sales at forty per cent. of revenue by 2029, from 26.1
per cent. in FY2025, and regards this issue as the first external funding of that shift. The Issuer does not intend to enter utility-scale
generation, where it would compete against much larger balance sheets, or to operate outside Malaysia.

4.7 Ranking of investors

The Sukuk constitute direct, unconditional, unsecured and unsubordinated obligations of the Issuer, ranking pari passu among themselves
and with its other unsecured and unsubordinated obligations, save for obligations mandatorily preferred by law.

The Sukuk rank behind secured financing of RM34.4 million to the extent of the assets securing it, being the rooftop installations and the
factory and equipment of the Issuer.

In the engineering contracting business, subcontractors and suppliers hold contractual and in some cases statutory rights in respect of
works and materials, and performance bond providers hold counter-indemnities, all of which affect the practical recovery available to an
unsecured creditor.

Fractional interests rank equally with whole units and with the Anchor Investor allocation.

Before making an investment decision, prospective investors should carefully consider all the information contained in this document,

7.1 Risks relating to the Issuer and the Sukuk

Credit risk
The Sukuk represent unsecured or, where applicable, secured obligations of the Issuer alone. Payment of profit and the repayment of the
nominal value depend entirely upon the ability of the Issuer to generate sufficient cash flow from its operations, or to obtain sufficient
funds from other sources, at the relevant times. Neither the Trustee, the Platform through which this offering was originated and
distributed, nor any other party has guaranteed or assumed any obligation in respect of the payment obligations of the Issuer. In
particular, investors should note that this offering was not underwritten and that no arranger stands behind it.

A deterioration in the operating performance of the Issuer, an adverse change in its capital structure, the loss of one or more significant
customers or contracts, or an unexpected increase in its cost base could impair its ability to service the Sukuk. As at 31 December 2025,
the Group had total borrowings of RM44.4 million against total equity of RM36.8 million, representing a gross gearing ratio of 1.21 times
and total borrowings to EBITDA of 2.9 times.

Refinancing risk
The Sukuk are repayable in a single bullet payment at maturity, and the Issuer does not expect to accumulate a sinking fund sufficient to
retire the full nominal amount from operating cash flow alone. The Issuer therefore expects to refinance all or a substantial part of the
Sukuk at or before maturity. The ability of the Issuer to refinance will depend on conditions in the Malaysian debt capital and bank loan
markets at the relevant time, on its credit standing and rating at that time, and on investor appetite for the sector in which it operates.
There is no assurance that refinancing will be available on acceptable terms, or at all.

Profit rate risk
The Sukuk carry a fixed profit rate. The market value of fixed-rate debt securities generally moves inversely with prevailing market yields:
if benchmark yields, including the yields on Malaysian Government Securities and Government Investment Issues, rise after the issue date,
the market value of the Sukuk is likely to fall. Investors who sell the Sukuk before maturity may therefore realise less than the nominal
value, and in adverse conditions materially less. Investors who hold the Sukuk to maturity are not exposed to this price risk, but bear the
opportunity cost of being locked into a fixed profit rate for the tenure of the instrument.

The Issuer is separately exposed to profit rate risk on its floating-rate bank borrowings, which are typically priced against the relevant cost
of funds of the lending institutions. An increase in the Overnight Policy Rate set by the central bank would increase the finance costs of the
Group and reduce the headroom available under its profit cover covenant.

Liquidity risk of the Sukuk
The Sukuk will not be listed or quoted on any stock exchange, and the Platform does not operate an order book. Operating a venue that
matches buyers and sellers of capital market products would require registration as a Recognized Market Operator, which the Platform has
not yet obtained. Exit before maturity is therefore available only through the standing bid maintained by the Anchor Investor at the
published formula price described in Section 9.5. That bid is a contractual undertaking of a single counterparty, not a market. It is capped
at RM140,000 of nominal value in aggregate in any calendar month, may be suspended in the circumstances set out in Section 9.5, and
terminates if the Anchor Investor ceases to hold at least half of its original allocation. Peer-to-peer transfer between investors is intended
to follow in phase two once Recognized Market Operator registration is obtained, but no date is committed and investors should not
subscribe in reliance on it. An investor who may need the money before 12 August 2031 should treat this offering as unsuitable.

Liquidity and working capital risk of the Issuer
The Issuer requires liquidity to fund its operations, service its debt and meet its capital commitments. As at 31 December 2025, the Group
held cash and bank balances of RM12.6 million and recorded a current ratio of 1.67 times. An unexpected lengthening of the collection
cycle, the withdrawal of uncommitted banking lines, or a requirement to post additional performance security could strain the liquidity
position of the Group.

Rating risk
The Sukuk have been assigned a rating of BBB+ with a stable outlook by Malaysian Rating Corporation Berhad. A credit rating is an
opinion on relative creditworthiness at a point in time and is not a recommendation to buy, sell or hold any security. The rating may be
reviewed, placed on negative watch, downgraded or withdrawn at any time. A downgrade would be likely to reduce the price obtainable on
a sale before maturity.

7.2 Risks relating to the industry and operations of the Issuer

Order book dependence in the contracting business

Engineering, procurement and construction work contributed RM62.8 million, or 66.7 per cent., of FY2025 revenue. That revenue exists
only for as long as the Issuer keeps winning contracts, and the order book stood at RM48.4 million as at 31 December 2025, representing
approximately nine months of contracting revenue.

Investors should register that figure. A contracting business with nine months of visibility can deteriorate very quickly if tendering
conditions change, and the Issuer competes against both larger integrators and low-cost entrants. Contract margins have been compressed
by competition, with the gross margin on contracting work declining from 19.4 per cent. to 17.8 per cent. over the review period.

Execution, warranty and performance guarantee risk

The Issuer typically warrants system output for two years and workmanship for five, and in some contracts provides a performance
guarantee under which it compensates the client if generation falls below a modelled level. Underperformance arising from design error,
installation defect or module degradation exceeding warranty limits falls on the Issuer.

Warranty claims on modules and inverters are passed to manufacturers, but this depends on those manufacturers remaining solvent, and
several global module makers have entered distress in recent years. The Issuer carried a warranty provision of RM1.8 million as at 31
December 2025.

Module and inverter price and supply volatility
Module prices have moved by more than thirty per cent. in a single year on multiple occasions over the past decade, driven by global
manufacturing capacity, trade measures and shipping costs. On fixed-price contracts already signed, a price increase falls on the Issuer.

Conversely, a sharp price decline can render inventory and signed contracts uncompetitive against later bidders. Inventories stood at
RM9.6 million as at 31 December 2025, and the Issuer bears price risk on that stock.

Offtaker credit risk on corporate power purchase agreements
The owned portfolio sells energy to commercial and industrial hosts under agreements of eight to eighteen years remaining term. Those
counterparties are private companies, not a national utility, and their credit is materially weaker.

An offtaker insolvency ends the revenue from that installation and leaves the Issuer with solar equipment on a roof it does not own, which
has limited value and may be costly to remove. The portfolio comprised 46 installations across 38 offtakers as at 31 December 2025, so no
single loss is critical, but the exposure is to a class of counterparty that fails more often than a utility does.

Solar resource variability and construction risk on the funded portfolio
Energy revenue depends on irradiance actually received, which varies with cloud cover, monsoon and haze episodes, and the Issuer is paid
only for energy delivered with no compensating mechanism.

The 12 MWac portfolio funded by this issue carries construction, roof condition, grid connection and commissioning risk, and is not
expected to be fully commissioned before 2029, while profit payments begin in February 2027.

Working capital intensity and contract receivables
Contracting consumes working capital: the Issuer buys modules, pays subcontractors and completes milestones before it is certified and
paid. Contract receivables and retention sums stood at RM28.2 million as at 31 December 2025, being 82 per cent. of the level of
contracting cost of sales for a quarter.

Retention sums are typically released only after the defects liability period expires. A dispute or a client insolvency can leave the Issuer

What is the profit rate, and how is it determined?

The profit rate is 6.80 per cent. per annum on nominal value, fixed for five years. That is RM3.40 per RM100 held every six months, or
RM6.80 a year. The rate reflects two things: a BBB+ rating, which is the lowest investment grade band and one notch above sub-
investment grade, and the fact that the Issuer is principally an engineering contractor rather than an owner of contracted energy assets.
Contracting revenue depends on winning new work; energy sales under long-term agreements do not.

How often are payments made, and when will I receive them?

Profit is paid semi-annually in arrears in arrears on 12 February and 12 August of each year, with the first payment falling due on 12
February 2027 and the final payment falling due on the maturity date, 12 August 2031. Payments are made to the persons shown in the
records of the depository at the close of business on the relevant record date, which falls two business days before the payment date.
Payments are credited directly through RENTAS; no physical certificates are issued and no coupons need to be presented.

When will I get my principal back?

The full RM100 per RM100 of nominal value is repaid on 12 August 2031 together with the final profit payment. There is no amortisation.
The Issuer may call the whole issue from the third anniversary at a small premium.

What happens if the Issuer defaults?

A default arises if the Issuer fails to pay any amount due within the applicable grace period, breaches a financial covenant, breaches
another material obligation and fails to remedy it within the cure period, becomes subject to insolvency proceedings, or triggers one of the
other events of default described in Section 4.9. Upon a continuing event of default, the Trustee may, and must if directed by an
extraordinary resolution of Sukukholders, accelerate the Sukuk so that the full nominal value and accrued profit become immediately due
and payable.

The Sukuk are unsecured and rank behind RM34.4 million of secured financing charged over the rooftop installations and the factory.
What Sukukholders would look to is the assignment of corporate power purchase agreement receivables on the unencumbered part of the
portfolio, RM6.4 million annually, the debt service reserve of RM272,000 held by the Trustee, and any residual value in the charged assets
above the secured claims. Investors should note that a solar installation on a third party's roof is difficult to realise independently of the
offtaker relationship, so its recovery value is materially below its book value. The contracting business would have little realisable value in
a default, since its value lies in an order book that would not survive the insolvency of the contractor.

Individual investors do not enforce directly. The Trustee acts on behalf of all Sukukholders collectively, which prevents a disorderly race to
enforce and ensures that any recoveries are distributed rateably in accordance with the priority of payments set out in the Trust Deed.

Is this issuance Shariah compliant?

Yes. The structure is Murabahah effected through a Tawarruq arrangement, and renewable generation is regarded as an inherently

are separately assessed against the SRI framework of the Issuer by Verdant Assurance Partners Sdn Bhd. Investors should consult their

What are the main risks I should focus on?

expects to refinance rather than fully amortise from operating cash flow. Third, profit rate risk: the rate is fixed, so the market value of the
Sukuk will fall if benchmark yields rise. Fourth, liquidity risk: the Sukuk are unlisted, secondary market trading may be thin, and investors
should be prepared to hold to maturity. Fifth, the sector-specific risks described in Section 7.2, which for the Issuer principally concern
dependence on an engineering order book that must be continually replenished, execution and warranty risk on installed systems, module

prices off the credit spread at which this issue was priced, not the spread prevailing after a downgrade, so the effect on the achievable exit
price is limited but the underlying credit will have deteriorated.

9.5 The standing bid, and its limits

The Anchor Investor has undertaken to the Trustee to maintain a standing bid for sukuk held by retail investors, at a price determined by
the formula published on the Platform and set out in the Standing Bid Agreement, which is available for inspection under Section 10.3.

The formula prices the sukuk by discounting the remaining scheduled payments at a reference yield, being the yield on Malaysian
Government Securities of comparable remaining tenure plus the credit spread at which this issue was priced, and then applying a discount
of 1.50 per cent. to reflect the cost of the Anchor Investor providing the facility. The formula is mechanical and is not a negotiation.

Investors should be clear about four limitations:

    The price is not the nominal value. Because the formula responds to prevailing yields, an investor selling into the standing bid when
    yields have risen will receive less than RM100 per RM100 of nominal value, and in adverse conditions materially less. The discount of
    1.50 per cent. applies in every case, including when yields have not moved.
    The bid is capped. The Anchor Investor is obliged to purchase not more than RM140,000 of nominal value in aggregate in any calendar
    month. If retail investors seek to sell more than that in a month, requests are met in the order received and the balance carries to the
    following month.
    The bid may be suspended. The undertaking is suspended while an event of default is continuing, during the fifteen business days
    before and after a periodic payment date, and where the Anchor Investor is prevented from dealing by applicable law. Investors should
    note that the first of these means the standing bid is least likely to be available at precisely the moment they most want to sell.
    The bid terminates. The undertaking ends if the Anchor Investor ceases to hold at least half of its original allocation, on its insolvency,
    or at the maturity date. If it terminates before maturity, investors have no exit mechanism at all until Recognized Market Operator
    registration is obtained, and there is no assurance as to when that will occur.

The Trustee monitors compliance with the Standing Bid Agreement and reports on it in the periodic reporting published through the

Investors should treat these limits as a floor on prudence rather than a substitute for it. The limits are set by the Platform and may be
