1. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

1.1 Overview of the Issuer

Gerak Logistik Bhd provides temperature-controlled logistics in Malaysia. It operates two cold store facilities, at Klang in Selangor and
Senai in Johor, with combined capacity of 28,400 pallet positions, and a fleet of 168 refrigerated vehicles. It serves food manufacturers,
supermarket groups and pharmaceutical distributors, and holds Good Distribution Practice accreditation for pharmaceutical distribution
and JAKIM halal certification at both facilities.

The Issuer has been listed on the Main Market of Bursa Malaysia since September 2010 and had a market capitalisation of RM196.4
million as at 30 June 2026. It employed 1,284 persons as at 31 December 2025.

For the financial year ended 31 December 2025 the Issuer recorded revenue of RM184.2 million, EBITDA of RM33.8 million and profit for
the financial year of RM11.8 million. Total assets stood at RM225.5 million and shareholders' funds at RM89.8 million. It has been
profitable in each of the last eighteen financial years, including through the pandemic.

1.2 Purpose of the offering

The Issuer is offering RM15,000,000 in nominal value of Sukuk Murabahah, the inaugural issue under its RM80.0 million Sukuk
Programme. Of that, RM10,500,000 is allocated to the Anchor Investor and RM4,500,000 to retail investors through the Tokenkan platform
in fractional denominations from RM100, subject to a maximum holding of RM30,000 per investor.

Proceeds will fund cold store expansion at Klang and Senai, replacement of 32 refrigerated vehicles, refinancing and working capital.
Section 5 has the detail.

1.3 The lowest-risk offering on the Platform, and what that does and does not mean

On every credit measure this is the strongest of the six issuers on the Platform: an A2 rating, gross gearing of 1.03 times against a 1.75
times covenant, EBITDA coverage of finance costs at 4.3 times against a 2.50 times minimum, unencumbered asset cover of 214 per cent.
against a 150 per cent. requirement, and eighteen consecutive profitable years.

It is also the offering with the lowest profit rate, at 5.90 per cent. That relationship is the whole point: on this Platform, rate and risk move
together, and an investor choosing between 5.90 per cent. here and 7.50 per cent. on the unrated offerings is being asked to price that
difference.

What the strength does not mean is that the instrument is safe in absolute terms. It is unsecured and ranks behind RM66.0 million of
secured financing. Nothing guarantees the A2 rating holds for five years. There is no investor put, so an investor needing money before
June 2031 depends on a standing bid that is capped at RM225,000 monthly and can be suspended. And the business requires continuous
capital expenditure, so free cash flow is thin even in good years.

1.4 Why this issuance exists

A conventional RM15.0 million bond issuance sits at the margin of viability in Malaysia. Fixed costs of RM1.4 million to RM1.9 million,
largely irrespective of size, would consume 9 to 13 per cent. of the proceeds. The total cost of this issuance is RM280,000, being 1.87 per
cent. of nominal value, the lowest proportion among the six offerings, which is what scale achieves even at this size.

2. ISSUER BACKGROUND

2.1 History and development

The Issuer was incorporated in June 2003 by Loh Kim Seng, who had spent the preceding decade in refrigerated haulage, initially
operating a fleet of twelve refrigerated trucks serving food manufacturers in the Klang Valley.

It commissioned its first cold store at Klang in 2008, which converted it from a haulier into a logistics operator with warehousing capability
and materially improved the stickiness of its customer relationships. It listed on the Main Market in September 2010, raising RM42.4
million, which funded expansion of that facility.

The Senai facility was commissioned in 2016 to serve Johor and the Singapore border trade. Good Distribution Practice accreditation for
pharmaceutical distribution was obtained in 2019, opening a higher-margin segment which contributed RM14.6 million in FY2025. Halal
logistics certification was obtained at both facilities in 2021.

This is the inaugural debt capital markets issuance of the Issuer, which has funded itself to date through equity, bank term financing and
hire purchase.

2.2 Business model

The Issuer earns revenue from three related activities. Temperature-controlled distribution moves chilled and frozen goods from
manufacturers and importers to supermarket distribution centres, food service operators and pharmacies. Cold storage warehousing holds
goods at specified temperatures, charged on pallet positions occupied and on handling movements. Ambient distribution and freight
forwarding serve the non-temperature-controlled needs of the same customers.

The economics rest on two things. The first is cold store utilisation, currently 91 per cent.: refrigeration energy, insulation maintenance
and standby power are largely fixed, so incremental pallet occupancy converts to margin at a high rate, and the reverse is equally true. The
second is fleet utilisation and route density, since a refrigerated vehicle running partly empty carries nearly the same cost as a full one.

The barriers that protect the business are accreditation and switching cost rather than scale. Good Distribution Practice accreditation and
halal certification take time to obtain and are audited continuously, and a customer moving its cold chain provider risks temperature
integrity during transition. Those barriers are real but they are not permanent, and each contract is exposed at re-tender.

2.6 Strategy

The strategy has three elements: to expand cold store capacity, currently 91 per cent. utilised, which is the binding constraint on growth;
to grow the pharmaceutical segment, where accreditation limits competition and margins are higher; and to maintain gearing below 1.25
times so the Issuer retains capacity to fund fleet replacement without recourse to equity. The Issuer does not intend to operate outside
Malaysia beyond its existing cross-border movements, or to enter ambient warehousing at scale.

whom at least two shall be independent, and to procure that the Audit and Risk Committee continues to be chaired by an independent

ambient warehouse units from a company connected to the Managing Director; both leases were independently valued and approved in
accordance with the Main Market Listing Requirements, and the terms are disclosed in the annual report.

4.4 Maturity structure

The Sukuk are structured as a single bullet maturity. The full nominal value of RM15,000,000 falls due on 25 June 2031 together with the
final profit payment. The Issuer intends to meet this from accumulated operating cash flow and refinancing. Investors should note that free
cash flow after capital expenditure was minus RM200,000, RM1.2 million and RM2.4 million in the three years under review, so
accumulated cash alone would not retire the issue and refinancing is expected to be required. The Issuer does, however, own cold store
property and fleet against which refinancing can be raised.

4.5 Early redemption

The Sukuk incorporate the following early redemption provisions:

Optional early redemption (call)
The Issuer may, on any profit payment date falling on or after the third anniversary of the issue date, redeem all but not part only of the

Mandatory redemption on disposal of a cold store
If the Issuer disposes of either the Klang or the Senai cold store facility, the net proceeds shall be applied within ninety days to the

Redemption following a change of control
Upon a change of control, being an event whereby Gerak Holdings Sdn Bhd and the Loh family cease collectively to hold, directly or

Redemption for taxation or Shariah reasons

No investor put

4.7 Ranking of investors

The Sukuk constitute direct, unconditional, unsecured and unsubordinated obligations of the Issuer, ranking pari passu among themselves
and with its other unsecured and unsubordinated obligations, save for obligations mandatorily preferred by law.

The Sukuk rank behind secured financing of RM66.0 million to the extent of the assets securing it, being the Klang and Senai cold store
facilities and the refrigerated fleet financed under hire purchase.

In respect of any shortfall, Sukukholders would rank as unsecured creditors behind winding-up costs and preferential claims including
employee entitlements. Investors should note that the Issuer employed 1,284 persons as at 31 December 2025, the largest workforce
among the issuers on the Platform, so preferential employee claims would be substantial.

Fractional interests rank equally with whole units and with the Anchor Investor allocation.

7.1 Risks relating to the Issuer and the Sukuk

Credit risk
The Sukuk represent unsecured or, where applicable, secured obligations of the Issuer alone. Payment of profit and the repayment of the
nominal value depend entirely upon the ability of the Issuer to generate sufficient cash flow from its operations, or to obtain sufficient
funds from other sources, at the relevant times. Neither the Trustee, the Platform through which this offering was originated and
distributed, nor any other party has guaranteed or assumed any obligation in respect of the payment obligations of the Issuer. In
particular, investors should note that this offering was not underwritten and that no arranger stands behind it.

A deterioration in the operating performance of the Issuer, an adverse change in its capital structure, the loss of one or more significant
customers or contracts, or an unexpected increase in its cost base could impair its ability to service the Sukuk. As at 31 December 2025,
the Group had total borrowings of RM92.2 million against total equity of RM89.8 million, representing a gross gearing ratio of 1.03 times
and total borrowings to EBITDA of 2.7 times.

Refinancing risk
The Sukuk are repayable in a single bullet payment at maturity, and the Issuer does not expect to accumulate a sinking fund sufficient to
retire the full nominal amount from operating cash flow alone. The Issuer therefore expects to refinance all or a substantial part of the
Sukuk at or before maturity. The ability of the Issuer to refinance will depend on conditions in the Malaysian debt capital and bank loan
markets at the relevant time, on its credit standing and rating at that time, and on investor appetite for the sector in which it operates.
There is no assurance that refinancing will be available on acceptable terms, or at all.

Profit rate risk
The Sukuk carry a fixed profit rate. The market value of fixed-rate debt securities generally moves inversely with prevailing market yields:
if benchmark yields, including the yields on Malaysian Government Securities and Government Investment Issues, rise after the issue date,
the market value of the Sukuk is likely to fall. Investors who sell the Sukuk before maturity may therefore realise less than the nominal
value, and in adverse conditions materially less. Investors who hold the Sukuk to maturity are not exposed to this price risk, but bear the
opportunity cost of being locked into a fixed profit rate for the tenure of the instrument.

The Issuer is separately exposed to profit rate risk on its floating-rate bank borrowings, which are typically priced against the relevant cost
of funds of the lending institutions. An increase in the Overnight Policy Rate set by the central bank would increase the finance costs of the
Group and reduce the headroom available under its profit cover covenant.

Liquidity risk of the Sukuk
The Sukuk will not be listed or quoted on any stock exchange, and the Platform does not operate an order book. Operating a venue that
matches buyers and sellers of capital market products would require registration as a Recognized Market Operator, which the Platform has
not yet obtained. Exit before maturity is therefore available only through the standing bid maintained by the Anchor Investor at the
published formula price described in Section 9.5. That bid is a contractual undertaking of a single counterparty, not a market. It is capped
at RM225,000 of nominal value in aggregate in any calendar month, may be suspended in the circumstances set out in Section 9.5, and
terminates if the Anchor Investor ceases to hold at least half of its original allocation. Peer-to-peer transfer between investors is intended
to follow in phase two once Recognized Market Operator registration is obtained, but no date is committed and investors should not
subscribe in reliance on it. An investor who may need the money before 25 June 2031 should treat this offering as unsuitable.

Liquidity and working capital risk of the Issuer
The Issuer requires liquidity to fund its operations, service its debt and meet its capital commitments. As at 31 December 2025, the Group
held cash and bank balances of RM19.8 million and recorded a current ratio of 1.19 times. An unexpected lengthening of the collection
cycle, the withdrawal of uncommitted banking lines, or a requirement to post additional performance security could strain the liquidity
position of the Group.

Rating risk
The Sukuk have been assigned a rating of A2 with a stable outlook by RAM Rating Services Berhad. A credit rating is an opinion on relative

maturity.

is disproportionate to the revenue effect. The Issuer lost one account representing 4.6 per cent. of revenue on re-tender in 2024 and
replaced it within nine months, which indicates the market is contestable in both directions.

Fuel and wage cost with limited pass-through

Diesel and driver wages are the two largest operating costs. Contracts contain fuel adjustment mechanisms on approximately sixty per
cent. of distribution revenue, typically reviewed quarterly with a lag, so a rapid fuel price increase is absorbed by the Issuer for one
quarter and only partly recovered thereafter.

Wage costs have no comparable mechanism. Minimum wage increases and competition for licensed drivers have raised driver cost per
kilometre in each year under review, and the Issuer cannot reprice mid-contract. The EBITDA margin nonetheless improved from 16.8 to
18.3 per cent. on scale and utilisation, but investors should not extrapolate that improvement.

Temperature excursion and product loss liability

The core commitment of a cold chain operator is that goods remain within a specified temperature range from collection to delivery. A
refrigeration failure, power interruption, delayed delivery or human error can spoil an entire consignment, and the Issuer bears liability for
the value of the goods, which for pharmaceutical consignments can substantially exceed the freight revenue earned.

The Issuer maintains goods-in-transit and warehouse cover of RM12.0 million per occurrence and recorded claims of RM840,000,
RM620,000 and RM710,000 over the review period. A single major excursion at a cold store, as opposed to in transit, would exceed those
figures and could jeopardise a large customer relationship as well.

Continuous capital expenditure
Investing outflows were RM18.6 million, RM20.4 million and RM22.8 million against operating inflows of RM18.4 million, RM21.6 million
and RM25.2 million, leaving free cash flow of minus RM200,000, RM1.2 million and RM2.4 million.

The greater part of this is replacement rather than growth. A fleet of 168 refrigerated vehicles on an eight-year cycle requires roughly 21
replacements annually irrespective of growth, and refrigeration plant at the cold stores requires periodic overhaul. Deferring this
expenditure would flatter cash flow while raising breakdown and excursion risk, so investors should regard it as non-discretionary.

Competition from larger integrated operators
The Issuer competes against larger regional and international logistics groups with broader networks, lower funding costs and the ability
to bundle cold chain with freight forwarding and customs services.

Its defence is specialisation, halal and Good Distribution Practice accreditation, and long-standing customer relationships. None of these is
permanent, and re-tendering exposes each account to a larger competitor at intervals of two to four years.

Cold store expansion execution and utilisation
RM7.4 million of this issue funds cold store expansion at Klang and Senai. The expansion is not fully contracted against committed volume:
approximately 62 per cent. of the additional pallet capacity is covered by letters of intent from existing customers, and the balance is
speculative.

Cold store space carries high fixed running costs, principally refrigeration energy, whether occupied or not. Unlet capacity is therefore
more costly than empty dry warehousing would be.

Refrigerant transition cost

Malaysia is phasing down hydrofluorocarbon refrigerants under international commitments. A proportion of the refrigeration plant of the
Issuer uses refrigerants subject to that phase-down and will require conversion or replacement over the coming decade.

What is the profit rate, and how is it determined?

Profit is paid semi-annually in arrears in arrears on 25 June and 25 December of each year, with the first payment falling due on 25
