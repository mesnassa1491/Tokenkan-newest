1. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

1.1 Overview of the Issuer

Lembah Impian Development Bhd is a Malaysian residential property developer operating in Negeri Sembilan and southern Selangor,
principally at Nilai and Seremban. It develops landed residential and shoplot products aimed at owner-occupier and first-time purchasers,
and retains a small portfolio of shoplot investment properties at Sepang.

The Issuer has delivered 2,140 units since 2004 and holds a land bank of 218 acres. It has been listed on the Main Market of Bursa
Malaysia since October 2012 and had a market capitalisation of RM142.6 million as at 30 June 2026. It employed 142 persons as at 31
December 2025.

For the financial year ended 31 December 2025 the Issuer recorded revenue of RM126.8 million, EBITDA of RM24.8 million and profit for
the financial year of RM10.3 million. Total assets stood at RM331.4 million and shareholders' funds at RM108.5 million.

1.2 Purpose of the offering

The Issuer is offering RM12,000,000 in nominal value of Sukuk Murabahah, the inaugural issue under its RM60.0 million Sukuk
Programme. Of that, RM8,400,000 is allocated to the Anchor Investor and RM3,600,000 to retail investors through the Tokenkan platform
in fractional denominations from RM100, subject to a maximum holding of RM25,000 per investor.

Proceeds will fund infrastructure and earthworks for Lembah Impian phase 3, construction working capital, the refinancing of a bridging
facility and working capital. Section 5 has the detail.

1.3 The one thing investors should read before anything else

The three financial years in this document do not show a growth trend, and it would be a mistake to read them as one. Revenue fell from
RM118.4 million in FY2023 to RM104.2 million in FY2024 before recovering to RM126.8 million in FY2025. Profit fell from RM9.4 million
to RM3.0 million before recovering to RM10.3 million.

This is ordinary for a developer of this size. Earnings are recognised as construction progresses, so a trough appears whenever one
development completes before the next reaches the stages producing material revenue. Investors should assume a comparable trough
within the four-year tenure of this issue.

What matters is what a trough does to the covenants. In FY2024, EBITDA cover of finance costs was 2.1 times against the 2.00 times
minimum these Sukuk require, and financing to EBITDA was 9.2 times against the 8.00 times maximum. Had this issue been
outstanding in FY2024, the Issuer would have breached its leverage covenant. On the enlarged post-issue debt, a repeat of that
year would breach both tests. Section 6.4 sets this out in full.

That is not a reason to avoid the offering, but it is the reason the rate is 6.20 per cent. rather than lower, and investors should size their
holding accordingly.

1.4 Why this issuance exists

A conventional RM12.0 million bond issuance is not economic in Malaysia. Fixed costs of RM1.4 million to RM1.9 million, largely
irrespective of size, would consume 12 to 16 per cent. of the proceeds. The total cost of this issuance is RM260,000, being 2.17 per cent. of
nominal value, achieved by removing the underwriter and standardising the documentation and registry functions.

2. ISSUER BACKGROUND

2.1 History and development

The Issuer was incorporated in July 2004 by Cheah Boon Hock, a civil engineer, and began with a 96-unit terraced scheme at Nilai. It built
steadily through the 2000s in the Nilai corridor, which it selected for its proximity to the North-South Expressway and to the industrial
employment base of the area, and which remains its principal asset.

The greater part of the present land bank was acquired between 2009 and 2017, largely from plantation owners and at prices well below
current levels. Management regards this historic land cost as the reason it can compete on price against developers holding more recently
assembled sites.

The Issuer listed on the Main Market of Bursa Malaysia in October 2012, raising RM38.4 million. The Sepang shoplot investment
properties were acquired in 2018 to introduce a recurring income stream. The Seremban scheme launched in 2021.

This is the inaugural debt issuance of the Issuer, which has funded itself to date through equity, project financing and bank borrowing.

2.2 Business model

The Issuer acquires land, obtains planning and development approvals, appoints third-party main contractors under fixed-price contracts,
sells units off-plan under statutory sale and purchase agreements, and recognises revenue as construction progresses.

Three variables drive the economics. Land cost, which for the Issuer is largely historic and low. Sales rate, which determines how quickly
capital is recovered and which depends on purchaser access to mortgage financing. And construction cost, transferred to contractors
under fixed-price contracts for as long as those contractors remain solvent.

The cash profile follows from this. Land and infrastructure are paid for at the outset, construction is funded progressively against
purchaser progress payments and project financing, and surplus is released only as a development completes. A developer consumes cash
when launching and generates it when completing, which is why the operating cash flow of the Issuer moved from RM14.2 million to
RM8.6 million and then to RM22.4 million over three years.

capacity of the Issuer; to grow recurring investment property income as a partial offset to development cyclicality; and to keep gearing

4.6 Security and collateral

The Sukuk are unsecured obligations of the Issuer. Investors should understand why no security was granted and what stands in its place.

A property developer finances each development by charging the land and work in progress of that development to a project financier. By
the time an issue of this kind is contemplated, the assets capable of bearing security are already charged. As at 31 December 2025 secured
Islamic financing amounted to RM116.6 million out of total financing of RM151.2 million, or 77.1 per cent. Granting security for these
Sukuk would have required either a second-ranking charge of little value or the release of a project financier, neither of which was
available.

The protections are therefore contractual:

a negative pledge preventing security over the present and future assets of the Issuer, save for permitted security. Investors should note
that permitted security expressly includes project financing security over new development sites, so this covenant does not freeze the
position and the Issuer will continue to charge new land ahead of Sukukholders;

an unencumbered asset covenant requiring consolidated assets not subject to security to represent at all times not less than one

hundred and thirty per cent. of aggregate unsecured financial indebtedness, tested semi-annually against the most recent valuation of

the Independent Valuer;

4.7 Ranking of investors

The Sukuk constitute direct, unconditional, unsecured and unsubordinated obligations of the Issuer, ranking pari passu among themselves
and with its other unsecured and unsubordinated obligations, save for obligations mandatorily preferred by law.

The Sukuk rank behind secured Islamic financing of RM116.6 million to the extent of the assets securing it, being the development land
and work in progress of the phase 1 and 2 developments, the shoplot investment properties, and the land securing the bridging facility.

The Sukuk are structurally subordinated to the creditors of the project subsidiaries through which developments are undertaken. The
Issuer depends on surplus distributions from those subsidiaries after their financiers have been discharged.

Investors should note the effect of Malaysian housing development legislation. Purchaser deposits and progress payments are held in
Housing Development Accounts applicable only to the development concerned, and purchasers hold statutory protections in respect of
them. As at 31 December 2025, RM8.6 million of the RM22.8 million cash balance was so held and is not available to Sukukholders.

Fractional interests rank equally with whole units and with the Anchor Investor allocation.

7.1 Risks relating to the Issuer and the Sukuk

Credit risk
The Sukuk represent unsecured or, where applicable, secured obligations of the Issuer alone. Payment of profit and the repayment of the
nominal value depend entirely upon the ability of the Issuer to generate sufficient cash flow from its operations, or to obtain sufficient
funds from other sources, at the relevant times. Neither the Trustee, the Platform through which this offering was originated and
distributed, nor any other party has guaranteed or assumed any obligation in respect of the payment obligations of the Issuer. In
particular, investors should note that this offering was not underwritten and that no arranger stands behind it.

A deterioration in the operating performance of the Issuer, an adverse change in its capital structure, the loss of one or more significant
customers or contracts, or an unexpected increase in its cost base could impair its ability to service the Sukuk. As at 31 December 2025,
the Group had total borrowings of RM151.2 million against total equity of RM108.5 million, representing a gross gearing ratio of 1.39
times and total borrowings to EBITDA of 6.1 times.

Refinancing risk
The Sukuk are repayable in a single bullet payment at maturity, and the Issuer does not expect to accumulate a sinking fund sufficient to
retire the full nominal amount from operating cash flow alone. The Issuer therefore expects to refinance all or a substantial part of the
Sukuk at or before maturity. The ability of the Issuer to refinance will depend on conditions in the Malaysian debt capital and bank loan
markets at the relevant time, on its credit standing and rating at that time, and on investor appetite for the sector in which it operates.
There is no assurance that refinancing will be available on acceptable terms, or at all.

Profit rate risk
The Sukuk carry a fixed profit rate. The market value of fixed-rate debt securities generally moves inversely with prevailing market yields:
if benchmark yields, including the yields on Malaysian Government Securities and Government Investment Issues, rise after the issue date,
the market value of the Sukuk is likely to fall. Investors who sell the Sukuk before maturity may therefore realise less than the nominal
value, and in adverse conditions materially less. Investors who hold the Sukuk to maturity are not exposed to this price risk, but bear the
opportunity cost of being locked into a fixed profit rate for the tenure of the instrument.

The Issuer is separately exposed to profit rate risk on its floating-rate bank borrowings, which are typically priced against the relevant cost
of funds of the lending institutions. An increase in the Overnight Policy Rate set by the central bank would increase the finance costs of the
Group and reduce the headroom available under its profit cover covenant.

Liquidity risk of the Sukuk
The Sukuk will not be listed or quoted on any stock exchange, and the Platform does not operate an order book. Operating a venue that
matches buyers and sellers of capital market products would require registration as a Recognized Market Operator, which the Platform has
not yet obtained. Exit before maturity is therefore available only through the standing bid maintained by the Anchor Investor at the
published formula price described in Section 9.5. That bid is a contractual undertaking of a single counterparty, not a market. It is capped
at RM180,000 of nominal value in aggregate in any calendar month, may be suspended in the circumstances set out in Section 9.5, and
terminates if the Anchor Investor ceases to hold at least half of its original allocation. Peer-to-peer transfer between investors is intended
to follow in phase two once Recognized Market Operator registration is obtained, but no date is committed and investors should not
subscribe in reliance on it. An investor who may need the money before 20 October 2030 should treat this offering as unsuitable.

Liquidity and working capital risk of the Issuer
The Issuer requires liquidity to fund its operations, service its debt and meet its capital commitments. As at 31 December 2025, the Group
held cash and bank balances of RM22.8 million and recorded a current ratio of 2.40 times. An unexpected lengthening of the collection
cycle, the withdrawal of uncommitted banking lines, or a requirement to post additional performance security could strain the liquidity
position of the Group.

Rating risk
The Sukuk have been assigned a rating of A3 with a stable outlook by RAM Rating Services Berhad. A credit rating is an opinion on relative
creditworthiness at a point in time and is not a recommendation to buy, sell or hold any security. The rating may be reviewed, placed on

maturity.

7.2 Risks relating to the industry and operations of the Issuer

Cyclicality and lumpy earnings
Development revenue is recognised as construction progresses and therefore depends on both sales rate and construction stage. An issuer
with a small number of concurrent projects cannot smooth those effects.

The three years presented demonstrate this directly: revenue of RM118.4 million, RM104.2 million and RM126.8 million, and profit of
RM9.4 million, RM3.0 million and RM10.3 million. Investors should read FY2024 as the informative year, not FY2025, and should assume a
comparable trough within the tenure of this issue.

In FY2024, EBITDA cover of finance costs fell to 2.1 times against the 2.00 times covenant applicable to these Sukuk, and financing to
EBITDA reached 9.2 times against the 8.00 times covenant. A repeat on the enlarged post-issue debt would breach both.

Sales rate and purchaser mortgage approval
The Issuer sells to owner-occupier and first-time purchasers in Negeri Sembilan and southern Selangor, most of whom require mortgage
financing. Sales conversion therefore depends on bank lending appetite, prevailing financing rates and household confidence rather than
on the efforts of the Issuer.

Booking-to-conversion averaged 64 per cent. over the review period, and the Issuer regards this as the most sensitive operating variable in
its business. A tightening of lending guidelines translates into cancelled bookings within weeks.

Unsold inventory and land holding cost
Property development costs and completed inventories stood at RM194.2 million as at 31 December 2025, being 58.6 per cent. of total
assets. Completed unsold units and undeveloped land generate no revenue while incurring financing cost, quit rent, assessment and
maintenance.

The Issuer holds a land bank of 218 acres in Negeri Sembilan with an estimated development period exceeding eight years at current
absorption rates. A prolonged downturn would extend that period and could require the write-down of land carried at cost.

Construction cost and contractor failure
The Issuer engages third-party main contractors under fixed-price contracts, which transfer cost inflation risk only while the contractor
remains solvent. Contractor failure mid-project causes delay, re-tendering at prevailing prices, and exposure to liquidated damages payable

The questions and answers below are provided for the convenience of prospective investors and are intended as a plain-language guide

What is the profit rate, and how is it determined?

The profit rate is 6.20 per cent. per annum on nominal value, fixed for four years. That is RM3.10 per RM100 held every six months, or
RM6.20 a year. Under the Murabahah structure the total profit is fixed at the outset and cannot vary with the performance of the Issuer.
The rate reflects the A3 rating, which sits toward the lower end of the investment grade range, and the cyclicality of property development
described in Section 7.2.

How often are payments made, and when will I receive them?

Profit is paid semi-annually in arrears in arrears on 20 April and 20 October of each year, with the first payment falling due on 20 April
2027 and the final payment falling due on the maturity date, 20 October 2030. Payments are made to the persons shown in the records of
the depository at the close of business on the relevant record date, which falls two business days before the payment date. Payments are
credited directly through RENTAS; no physical certificates are issued and no coupons need to be presented.

When will I get my principal back?

The full RM100 per RM100 of nominal value is repaid on 20 October 2030 together with the final profit payment. There is no amortisation.
The Issuer may call the whole issue from the second anniversary at a small premium.

What happens if the Issuer defaults?

A default arises if the Issuer fails to pay any amount due within the applicable grace period, breaches a financial covenant, breaches
another material obligation and fails to remedy it within the cure period, becomes subject to insolvency proceedings, or triggers one of the
other events of default described in Section 4.9. Upon a continuing event of default, the Trustee may, and must if directed by an
extraordinary resolution of Sukukholders, accelerate the Sukuk so that the full nominal value and accrued profit become immediately due
and payable.

The Sukuk are unsecured, so the Trustee would prove in a winding-up rather than enforce security, and investors should weigh three
features of a developer's balance sheet. First, 77.1 per cent. of the financing of the Issuer is secured on its land and work in progress and
ranks ahead. Second, purchaser monies in Housing Development Accounts, RM8.6 million as at 31 December 2025, are ring-fenced by
statute and unavailable to any creditor. Third, a partially built development is worth far less than a finished one, so recovery depends on
whether projects can be completed rather than liquidated. The unencumbered asset covenant at 130 per cent. of unsecured debt is the
principal protection, and the project account undertaking is intended to capture development surpluses as they are released.

Individual investors do not enforce directly. The Trustee acts on behalf of all Sukukholders collectively, which prevents a disorderly race to
enforce and ensures that any recoveries are distributed rateably in accordance with the priority of payments set out in the Trust Deed.

Is this issuance Shariah compliant?

Yes. The structure is Murabahah effected through a Tawarruq arrangement: Sukukholders, through the Trustee, purchase Shariah-
compliant commodities and sell them to the Issuer at a deferred price comprising nominal value plus an agreed profit. The deferred sale
price is the obligation owed to investors. The structure has been reviewed and approved by Ibnu Hakim Shariah Advisory Sdn Bhd.
Investors should consult their own Shariah advisers.

What are the main risks I should focus on?

The Issuer would direct attention to five risks in particular. First, credit risk: repayment depends entirely on the Issuer, and the Sukuk
benefit from no third-party guarantee. Second, refinancing risk: the Sukuk repay in a single bullet payment at maturity and the Issuer
expects to refinance rather than fully amortise from operating cash flow. Third, profit rate risk: the rate is fixed, so the market value of the
Sukuk will fall if benchmark yields rise. Fourth, liquidity risk: the Sukuk are unlisted, secondary market trading may be thin, and investors
should be prepared to hold to maturity. Fifth, the sector-specific risks described in Section 7.2, which for the Issuer principally concern the
cyclicality and lumpiness of development earnings, sensitivity of sales rates to interest rates and mortgage approval, construction cost
inflation and contractor failure, holding costs on unsold inventory and undeveloped land, dependence on planning and development
approvals, and liquidated damages exposure on late delivery. Section 7 should be read in full.

working capital, and RM260,000 to the expenses of the offering including the Platform origination fee.

What is the credit rating, and what does it mean?

RAM Rating Services Berhad has assigned a rating of A3 with a stable outlook to the Sukuk. On the rating scale of the agency, an A rating
denotes adequate capacity to meet financial obligations, with greater susceptibility to adverse changes in circumstances and economic
conditions than obligations rated in the AA and AAA categories. The numerical modifier 3 indicates the lower end of that band, immediately
above the BBB category and therefore toward the lower end of investment grade. A rating is an opinion on relative creditworthiness at a
given point in time. It is not a recommendation to buy, sell or hold, it does not comment on the market price or suitability of the instrument
for any particular investor, and it may be reviewed, downgraded or withdrawn at any time.

Investors should be clear about four limitations:

    The price is not the nominal value. Because the formula responds to prevailing yields, an investor selling into the standing bid when
